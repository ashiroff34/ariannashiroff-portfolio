### Bio (paste as a single text block, paragraphs separated by blank lines):
```
I was born in Munich and moved back to the States as a kid. The version of me that came out of that is somebody who notices when language shifts between rooms. When the same thing gets said one way to one audience and another way to a different one. When something is true in a clinic but not in a journal article. When someone uses a word that doesn't quite fit. A lot of what I do is sitting in those small mismatches and figuring out what's underneath them.

At 15 I co-founded Pangea Kids, a nonprofit getting medical supplies to clinics in Saigon. By the time I was Vice President, we had fifteen chapters across schools, $10K raised, and around two thousand items donated. What I actually learned was that the hard part of low-resource medicine isn't medical. It's all the stuff between the donor and the nurse: figuring out which clinic actually needs which supply, when, and how to get it there in usable condition. That's where most things break, and it's the part nobody puts in the brochure.

What I keep finding is that the technical part is usually the easier part. What breaks is the layer where a real person, in a real moment, has to actually use the thing. That layer almost never gets written down. So I write it down. That's most of what I've been working on, in one form or another, for years.

The other things about me: I'm a freshman at Penn State in the Schreyer Honors BS/MBA program. Five years, two degrees, science and business at the same time. Right now I'm preparing for a clinical trials internship at Pfizer this summer, building Thyself toward a public release, and trying to read at least one good thing every day. I lift, walk at sunset, write more than I sleep, and spend most of my best hours with people I love. If you're working on something where the technical part isn't the hard part, I'd love to talk.
```

# THEME / STYLING

In Google Sites' **Themes panel (top-right)**:
- Theme: **Modulor** or **Diplomat** (closest to vintage editorial)
- Font: **Display** = `Fraunces` (if available; fall back to `Playfair Display` or `Cormorant`)
- Font: **Body** = `Inter` or `Lato`
- Color: pick the warm/cream palette option

These won't get you the parchment-and-sepia look exactly — Google Sites doesn't allow custom CSS — but they'll get you closer than the default.
